# Magic Items Backlog

The third content wave: **40 magic items** for `items/magic/`. Written per the same three
spec docs (`docs/item-template.md`, `docs/style-guide.md`, `docs/pricing-guide.md`), plus the
rules below — magic items are the one category where combat math is allowed, so the
guardrails move rather than disappear.

**Status key:** ✅ merged · 🔨 claimed/in progress · ⬜ open

## The Three Rules of This Wave

1. **SRD effects only, or genuinely new small ones.** Either restate an SRD magic item's
   effect (a +1 weapon is a +1 weapon) or invent an effect small enough that a DM can
   adjudicate it cold. No new spell effects, no charges tables longer than a line, no
   attunement chains. If the mechanic needs a paragraph, it's too big for this wave.
2. **The quirk is the product.** Every item in this wave carries a *quirk* — a sensory or
   behavioral oddity with no mechanical weight (hums near undead, smells of cinnamon, the
   reflection lags). Quirks are what make an anonymous +1 memorable and what let a DM use
   the item as a plot sensor. Quirks go in the Description and At the Table, never in the
   mechanics block. Every quirk in the wave must be distinct.
3. **Price by the rarity band, then adjust for identity.** Use the pricing guide's magic
   table (common 50–100, uncommon 200–500, rare 2,000–10,000, very rare 20,000–50,000).
   Named/quirked items add 25–100% over an anonymous equivalent — most items in this wave
   are named, so most sit in the upper half of their band. Legendary items take no price
   field. Availability is `special-order` at rare and above.

## Brief Addendum for This Wave

Everything in the v0.1 writer brief applies, plus:

1. **Mechanics block stays short.** State the bonus or effect, the activation, and any
   limit-per-day. Nothing else. The quirk is not a mechanic.
2. **Every quirk distinct across the wave.** Before writing, list the quirks already used
   in merged magic files. No two items may share a sensory register (don't write three
   items that smell of something).
3. **Attunement:** use it only where an SRD analogue would require it. Note it in the 5e
   block in a single clause; PF2e uses invested items equivalently.
4. **Hooks carry provenance.** The best magic hooks are about *ownership*: who had it, how
   they lost it, who's still looking. The updated style guide's over-used shapes list
   applies — at most one per batch, and "the maker won't say" is nearly always the wrong
   choice for magic.
5. **Resale is a story.** Fill `resale` at ~50% of city price, but remember the pricing
   guide's note: collectors and auctions can exceed city price for named, storied items.
   That gap is where adventures live.
6. **Batch integrity covers mechanical shape here too:** no more than two items per batch
   may resolve to the same shape (once-per-day activation, passive sensor, object-property,
   passive continuous effect, record). **M2 is exempt from this rule for its `+1` baseline**
   — a batch of ten `+1` arms cannot satisfy it, and shouldn't have to. M2 carries its
   variety in quirk, provenance, and base weapon instead. The rule still binds M2 for any
   effect *beyond* the `+1`, and it binds M1, M3, and M4 in full.

Update this file's status column in the same PR that adds the items.
