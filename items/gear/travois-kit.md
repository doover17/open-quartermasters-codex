## Description

A bundle of oiled straps and woven webbing that looks like nothing until it meets two straight poles — then it becomes the shape every hunter and stretcher-bearer knows at a glance. The leather is stained dark at the shoulder loops where it rides against a working body for miles, and one strap has been chewed through and knotted back together by someone in a hurry.

## At the Table

- The statue is too heavy, the chest is too heavy, the fighter is too heavy — and the travois turns "we can't" into "we can, at half speed, leaving a track a blind man could follow."
- Dragging a wounded companion out over two days of bad country, pole-ends scoring the earth behind you, is the slowest and best kind of victory lap.

## Hooks

A hunter found travois tracks on the high trail: two clean pole-lines and boot prints, heading up the mountain, weighted going in. There is no returning track, no camp, and no season in living memory when anyone hauled a loaded travois toward the peak. The pole-lines stop at a rock face, mid-stride.
