## Description

Ivory-nibbed pens, a brass sighting compass that swings true and stops dead, and inks that smell faintly of walnut and iron — including one that shifts from blue to black as it dries, so you can see at a glance what was drawn today. The vellum sheets are pre-ruled with hairline graticules by a hand steadier than most. Tucked in the lid is a printed table of chart-house rates, folded and refolded to softness.

## At the Table

- The party stops being tourists and starts being an expedition: every hex they cross is now worth coin to somebody.
- A finished map is a negotiable document. Who gets to buy it — the duchy, the mining consortium, the people who live there — is a session.

## Hooks

A chart-house pays standing rates for surveys of a particular stretch of coast, and has for eleven years. It has bought the same coastline more than thirty times, from thirty different hands, and files every copy without comparing them. The clerk who accepts submissions is not curious, and is paid well not to be.
