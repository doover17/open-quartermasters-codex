## Description

It smells of vinegar, beeswax, and old paper — the smell of a records office with something to hide. Ink bottles are labeled not by color but by institution: customs black, chancery brown, a red the tag identifies only as "tax." Half the wax blanks are pristine; the other half carry test impressions of a seal that has been carefully gouged out of each one.

## At the Table

- The party doesn't fight past the checkpoint; they hand over papers and hold their breath while the guard reads.
- Restocking is its own scene — official paper isn't sold, it's stolen, and every scrivener who supplies it knows exactly what it's for.

## Hooks

One burin in the kit is worn to fit a stranger's grip, and the practice sheets rolled beneath the tray repeat a single name in a slowly improving hand. Records at the chancery say the person who owns that name signed a confession last spring. The signature on the confession is the best version on the sheet.
