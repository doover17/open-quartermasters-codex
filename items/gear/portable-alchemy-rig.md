## Description

Folded shut, it could pass for a courier's chest; open, it blooms into a bench — copper burner, nested retorts sleeved in wool, a rack of stoppered reagents that clink like distant bells when the mule stumbles. It smells of sulfur, mint, and scorched cork, and no amount of airing changes that. Someone has riveted a small silver plaque inside the lid and then, later, pried the name off it.

## At the Table

- The alchemist stops asking the party to wait in town. Every campsite becomes a workshop, and every workshop becomes a smell the whole camp votes on.
- Setting up the rig at an inn draws a crowd, a constable, or a customer — sometimes all three, in that order.

## Hooks

Rigs like this are commissioned, never stocked, and the workshop that builds them keeps a ledger of buyers. The last three entries in that ledger are written in the same hand as the orders they fulfilled — the buyers dictating, the clerk explained, though nobody remembers any of the three speaking.
