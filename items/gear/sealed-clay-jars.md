## Description

Fired river clay with a comfortable heft and a glaze that crazes into fine spiderwebs after a season's use. The wax seal peels up in one satisfying ribbon, and the smell underneath tells you immediately whether you're glad you opened it. Potters scratch their initials into the base while the clay is wet.

## At the Table

- Bulk logistics made cheap: water for the desert crossing, oil for the siege, or the pickled proof of what's been eating the livestock, carried home to someone who can identify it.
- Found sealed in a ruin, a jar is a small mystery with a built-in dramatic beat: the ribbon of wax, the pause, the smell.

## Hooks

A farmstead's cellar holds forty jars, all sealed by the same hand, all scratched with the same initials — and the family that lives there now can't say whose they are. They've been eating from the jars all winter. The seals at the back of the cellar are newer than the ones at the front.
