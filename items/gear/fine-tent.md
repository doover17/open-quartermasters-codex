## Description

Tight-woven sailcloth that goes drum-taut on its poles and stays that way — you can lay a palm against the wall in a gale and feel the wind pass over without a single snap of canvas. The seams are lapped and waxed the way shipwrights waterproof a hull, and one corner grommet has been re-sewn by a different, finer hand than the rest.

## At the Table

- Rain starts mid-scene, and the tent is up before the argument about whether to make camp is finished.
- In hostile country, an ordinary tent cracks and booms all night like a slow drum. This one lets the party sleep dark, quiet, and close to the thing they're stalking.

## Hooks

The sailmaker who sews these takes payment in coin like anyone else, but insists every buyer tell her where they intend to sleep first. She writes each answer in a ledger, and the ledger's early pages are crossed out in black, name by name.
