## Description

They smell of neatsfoot oil and nothing else — no new-leather stiffness, no creak when you flex the toe. The cobbler lasted them from a tracing of your bare feet, and the first step feels like the thousandth. The soles are stitched rather than pegged, and the welt is doubled at the toe where every other cobbler in the trade saves himself a stitch.

## At the Table

- A cobbler's masterwork is invisible: no break-in, no blisters, no repairs, no replacement. The character who buys these stops thinking about their feet for the rest of the campaign.
- Day six of the overland slog: everyone else is limping and airing blisters at the fire, and one character offers, unbearably, to take the second watch too.

## Hooks

The cobbler keeps a carved wooden last of every foot he has ever fitted, shelved and labeled, going back forty years. Last month a stranger paid him to make a pair from one of the old lasts — not their own — and paid triple to take the last with them when they left.
