## Description

They smell of neatsfoot oil and nothing else — no new-leather stiffness, no creak when you flex the toe. The cobbler lasted them from a tracing of your bare feet, and the first step feels like the thousandth. Where a heel should click on flagstone, a core of pressed felt lands like a held breath.

## At the Table

- Crossing the marble gallery past the sleeping guard, and the rogue looks down to discover the fighter is somehow right behind her.
- Day six of the overland slog: everyone else is limping and airing blisters at the fire, and one character offers, unbearably, to take the second watch too.

## Hooks

The cobbler keeps a carved wooden last of every foot he has ever fitted, shelved and labeled, going back forty years. Last month a stranger paid him to make a pair from one of the old lasts — not their own — and paid triple to take the last with them when they left.
