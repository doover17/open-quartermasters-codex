## Description

The sack smells of tar and old bacon fat no matter how new it is, and the cord is waxed stiff so wet bark won't bind it. A tin tag on the drawstring is stamped with a crude bear, teeth bared — the outfitter's joke, or warning. The throwing weight is a river stone in a leather net, worn glossy by a hundred cold-fingered tosses over a hundred branches.

## At the Table

- The nightly hang is a ritual that builds the wilderness: ten minutes of throwing, cursing, and hauling, and the forest feels real. The night the party skips it is the night the DM has been waiting for.
- Claw marks eight feet up the hanging tree, higher than yesterday's, turn a routine camp chore into the session's quietest dread.

## Hooks

In one valley the hangs have stopped working. Bags strung well above any bear's reach are found emptied — lowered, unpacked, and hauled back up, with the knots retied. Retied better.
