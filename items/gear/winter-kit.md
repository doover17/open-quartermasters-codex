## Description

It arrives as one dense roll that smells of cedar shavings and animal fat, heavier than any single garment has a right to be. The coat's fur faces inward, hide out, and the hood is cut deep enough to turn your face into a warm cave. Someone has already worn the mittens long enough to shape them — the furrier calls this "proofing," and doesn't say who does it.

## At the Table

- The pass is snowed in, the shortcut over the ridge saves four days, and the whole question is: who bought the kit and who said they'd be fine.
- Removing a mitten to work a lock, string a bow, or feel for a pulse — with the cold counting the seconds.

## Hooks

The furrier's stock is better this season than anyone can remember — deep pelts, whole hides, no trap scars. He buys from a valley three days north where, the drovers mention, the trapping families stopped coming to market in the spring. The pelts keep coming down anyway.
