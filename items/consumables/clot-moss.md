## Description

A dense russet moss, dried in flat wads that smell of peat smoke and iron. Pressed into a wound it swells, darkens, and grips like a living scab. Harvesters cut it from bog margins with wooden knives — steel, they insist, turns the batch, and no buyer has ever talked one out of the habit.

## At the Table

- No healer, no magic, blood in the grass: the pack in a belt pouch is the whole difference between a body and a survivor.
- The soured-wound clock gives a rescued patient a deadline. Getting them stable was the easy half.

## Hooks

The best clot-moss comes from one bog, the one the harvesters work in silence and leave before dusk. Ask why and they show you the wads that came up already dark and swollen, from ground where nothing had bled.
