## Description

Break the wax seal and the room fills with brandy, quince, and slow-roasted goose fat; underneath the preserving liquor, whole figs and glazed fowl and candied chestnuts sit in layers like sediment in a riverbank. It tastes of a harvest festival that someone bottled at its peak. Preservers build each jar over three days and sign the underside of the lid, and a signed lid from a dead preserver sells for more than the food ever did.

## At the Table

- The gift that opens the door: presented to a magistrate, a matriarch, or a rival captain, it says wealth, taste, and effort in one object.
- Cracking one open in a snowbound camp turns a grim night into a story the party tells for years.
- An unopened jar in a looted cellar poses its own question: eat it, sell it, or find out who it was meant for.

## Hooks

A widowed magistrate has received one jar on the same night each year since her husband died, left on the doorstep with no note. This year's jar arrived early, and the signature under the lid is her husband's.
