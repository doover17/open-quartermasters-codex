## Description

Finger-length strips of pale inner bark, bitter enough to pucker your whole jaw before the dull warmth spreads. Every pouch smells of riverbanks and rain. Old soldiers can tell upland bark from lowland by the aftertaste, and they argue about it the way other people argue about wine.

## At the Table

- On day four of the march, the character passing strips down the line is the one holding the party together.
- A bitten-down wad in a corpse's cheek says this person walked a long way in pain — and kept going.

## Hooks

The ferryman at the crossing buys pouches by the score, far more than one aching back needs. He pays in coin that's always damp.
