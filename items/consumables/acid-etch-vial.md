## Description

A brush built into the stopper, a liquid the green of old bottle glass, and a smell like vinegar that lost an argument. Painted onto iron it works without light, heat, or sound — the metal goes first dull, then lacy, then gone. Engravers invented it for lettering; certain customers taught it other trades, which is why honest sellers now scratch a tally of every vial into their ledger door.

## At the Table

- Ten silent minutes is the whole tension of an infiltration in one object: the guard's round takes eight.
- Buying one is easy; buying three in the same week starts a rumor with the party's description attached.

## Hooks

An engraver's apprentice has been underreporting the shop's tally by one vial a month for a year. The missing twelve would eat through the hinges of exactly one thing in this city: nobody keeps a door that large except the crypt under the counting house.
