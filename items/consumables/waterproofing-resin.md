## Description

A shallow tin of translucent amber paste that smells of pine tar and beeswax and warms to spreadable under a thumb. Brushed into leather or seam-stitching it sinks in and vanishes, leaving only a faint shine and the sound of rain giving up. Coastal cobblers score the tin's lid with the month of sale — a courtesy, and a quiet reminder to come back before the autumn storms.

## At the Table

- The river crossing goes wrong for everyone except the character whose spellbook comes out of the water dry.
- One tin before the wet season is cheap; remembering it during the wet season is character development.

## Hooks

A drowned courier washed ashore with everything ruined except the scroll case at her belt, sealed with a resin no chandler on the coast can identify. The letter inside is addressed to a person who insists, credibly, that she has been dead for forty years.
