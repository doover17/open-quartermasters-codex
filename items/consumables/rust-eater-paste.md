## Description

A shallow tin of gray-brown paste that stings the nose like vinegar poured over a forge. Worked into a seized hinge it fizzes, weeps orange, and leaves the metal bare, bright, and hungry-looking — smiths warn you to oil whatever it touches within the day. The underside of most tins carries a previous owner's scratch-tally of doors that owed it their opening.

## At the Table

- The sea-fort's every hinge is scaled orange; the party with a tin doesn't force doors, it schedules them — one fizzing minute apiece, quietly.
- A sword rusted into its scabbard, freed on the spot, hands a family heirloom back its dignity — and its edge.

## Hooks

The smith refuses to sell the paste to anyone bound for the drowned fort, and won't discuss it. The last tin that went out there came back with a peddler — scraped empty, and the owner's name filed off the lid.
