## Description

A doeskin pouch of powder the color of dried mustard, odorless to you, which is the unsettling part — your nose keeps reaching for it and finding nothing. It dusts on fine as pollen and deadens every smell you carry: sweat, woodsmoke, fear. Old huntsmen sew a pinch into their collars before wolf country, a habit their grandmothers called paying the toll.

## At the Table

- The baying behind the party goes confused, then circular, then silent — a chase scene ended by one pouch and a river the dogs never needed.
- Dusting the whole party before a warren crawl costs a handful of coin and buys an hour of walking past things with better noses than eyes.

## Hooks

Somewhere upriver is a hollow where nothing has a smell — not the moss, not the water, not the deer carcass that should have drawn flies a week ago.
