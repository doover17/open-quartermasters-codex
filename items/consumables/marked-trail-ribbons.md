## Description

Twenty strips of coarse linen boiled in dye and wax, stiff enough to crackle when you knot one to a branch. The color is a screaming madder-red that no leaf, lichen, or berry in any honest forest can match. Outfitters cut them from bolts woven for exactly this trade — where chalk washes off in the first rain, a ribbon hangs on and fades slow.

## At the Table

- The party's line of red knots is a map they wrote themselves — and anything following them can read it too.
- Finding a single ribbon of an unfamiliar color, deep in country the party believed unmapped, changes the expedition's whole posture.
- Untying your ribbons on the way out is an hour of work and a real decision: leave the road open, or close it behind you.

## Hooks

Three seasons of ribbon lines converge on the same ravine — three colors, three expeditions, none of whom ever met. None of them came back to collect their knots.
