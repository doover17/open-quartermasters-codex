## Description

An ash-hafted spear with a leaf blade and a collar of blued iron, and the haft frosts. Not in cold — in high summer, in a shuttered room, in the middle of a crowded hall, a white bloom of rime creeps up eight or ten inches of the wood and stands there for a minute or two before going to water. It has done this on battlefields and at breakfast, and nobody has ever made the pattern mean anything.

## At the Table

- The spear frosting mid-conversation is the cheapest suspense the DM owns: every player at the table will decide it means something, and they will decide different things.
- Hand it to a superstitious NPC and watch them read their own fortune into a piece of wet ash wood.

## Hooks

The spear turns up in three separate assize records as evidence, always in the same role: it stood in the corner of a room while someone was questioned, and in all three the accused confessed. The clerks note the frost each time, in the margin, in a shorthand they clearly expected the next clerk to understand. Two of the three confessions were later found to be false.
