## Description

Undyed road-wool, patched at both elbows by different hands in different decades, and it smells of nothing at all — not wool, not smoke, not the miles. Wearing it, distance stops accumulating in your legs. What it takes instead is small and specific: at the end of every journey, one place you passed through is gone from you entirely. Not dimmed. Gone, with the shape of it left behind, so you know the size of what you're missing.

## At the Table

- The cloak turns the overland slog into a non-problem, which is exactly what a party wants — until the session where they need to describe the village they walked through six days ago.
- Let the player choose what they lose, or don't. Both versions are good, and they are different games.

## Hooks

Its owners are easy to identify in the records because they all kept journals, obsessively, from the day they put it on. The last owner's journal runs to four volumes and is meticulous about inns, fords, and prices — and it has eleven pages cut out of it with a knife, at intervals of roughly a year. She cut them herself. The stubs are annotated, in her hand: *not this one either.*
