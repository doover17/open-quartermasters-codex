## Description

Two hand-sized tablets of dark slate in wooden frames, each with a stub of chalk on a string. Words arrive stroke by stroke as the other hand makes them, which means you watch your correspondent hesitate, cross out, and start again. Ink and chalk both dry at a normal pace on ordinary news and slow to a crawl on bad — a line about a death can stay wet for an hour, and every long-term user knows what a slow-drying slate means before they read it.

## At the Table

- Real-time correspondence across any distance changes what a split party can attempt. It also means the DM can deliver bad news mid-scene, one letter at a time.
- Watching the other person hesitate is the whole item. A sentence begun, erased, and replaced tells the party more than the sentence does.

## Hooks

Slates are sold in pairs and a pair outlives its owners, which is why courier offices buy up singles and hold them. One office keeps a shelf of nineteen unpaired slates under a cloth, and about once a season one of them writes something. The staff have standing instructions to copy down whatever appears, wipe the slate, and never write back. The book of copied lines is nearly full.
