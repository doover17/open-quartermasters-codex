## Description

A copper vane no bigger than a coin, hung on a fine chain, and it will not hang straight. Level the chain, weight the end, lay the whole thing flat on a table — it settles a few degrees off, every time, and always the same few degrees. When weather is coming you feel it before you see it: a slow cold turn in the metal against your breastbone, about an hour ahead of the sky agreeing with it.

## At the Table

- An hour is exactly enough to make camp, reef a sail, or decide to push on anyway. Weather stops being a DM announcement and becomes a party decision.
- Sailors, drovers, and siege engineers all want one and none of them can afford three, so the wearer becomes the person everyone consults — and blames.

## Hooks

Harbor masters pay well for these and log every warning acted on, because an hour saves ships. One port's log carries a single entry with no weather written beside it: the charm turned cold, the master cleared the quay, and the sky stayed flat and blue all afternoon. The wave came in at dusk out of that calm and took the empty quay with it.
