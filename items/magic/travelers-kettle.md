## Description

A dented tin kettle with a wired-on handle, the kind that costs two silver anywhere in the world. Water comes out of it clean and cold and perfectly clear — and tasting, faintly and briefly, of whatever it stopped being. Marsh water comes out tasting of iron and old leaves; cistern water of dust; and once in a while a kettle of river water comes up tasting of nothing at all, which the crews on the fever rivers say is the one to be careful of.

## At the Table

- The water problem is solved forever, which turns deserts, marshes, and sieges from survival math into terrain the party can cross at a walk.
- The aftertaste is the DM's free hand: what the water was carrying is a fact the party learns by drinking, one mouthful before anyone has thought to ask.

## Hooks

Mission-posts issue these along the fever rivers and take them back when a post closes, which is how this one came to market. Its previous post kept a taste-log, as the posts do, in a ruled book of dates and single words: *leaves, iron, dust, iron, leaves.* The last four months of entries are all the same word, and the word is *salt*, and that river is three hundred miles from any sea.
