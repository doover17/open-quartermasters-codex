## Description

A counting-frame of nine wires and ninety beads in a hardwood case, small enough to work one-handed. The beads are worn — deeply, unevenly, to the shape of thumbs — but the wear is on the wrong ones: the beads a counter's hand actually falls on are smooth and new, and the polished hollows sit in a sequence nobody has ever managed to reproduce.

## At the Table

- The customs shed, the sealed vault, the coffin at the crossroads: the party can now know before they open, which turns "should we?" into a much better question than "can we?"
- It doesn't say if a thing is alive. Reading "one man, one lantern, forty pounds of chain" off a sealed crate is a complete scene with no monster in it.

## Hooks

Harbor authorities in two cities have tried to buy this outright and been refused by a seller who does not appear to want money. What he wants, according to the clerk who handled both approaches, is a written undertaking that the tally will never be used on the cargo of a particular company — and both authorities refused that, and he kept the tally, and the company has not shipped anything through either port since.
