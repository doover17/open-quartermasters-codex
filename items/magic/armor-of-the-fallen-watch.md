## Description

Half plate in dark, close-fitted steel, the breast dented twice and never dressed out. Names surface in the metal the way frost surfaces on glass — thin bright letters that rise over a few days, hold for a season, and sink away again. They are the names of everyone who has worn it. Yours arrives about a week in, low on the left side, under the arm, where you cannot read it without help.

## At the Table

- Wearing it means asking somebody to read your own name off your ribs. That is a scene, and it happens once per owner.
- The names come and go, so the armor is a rotating cast the DM can deal from: whoever surfaced this month is somebody the party can now go and find, or bury.

## Hooks

Sixty-one names have been recorded off this armor by the garrisons that have held it, and the lists don't match. Four names appear on every list. Nine appear once and never again. And one name has surfaced in every single season since the records begin, in the same place on the right pauldron, belonging to a woman who by every account never wore the armor at all — she commissioned it.
