## Description

A round shield of limewood faced in boiled hide, its boss dented flat on one side, and it is warm — always, on both faces, the low steady warmth of a stone that has sat near a fire. Carry it into a house and it warms further, until under a roof with people in it you can feel it through a gambeson. Out on open ground it goes down to blood heat and stays there.

## At the Table

- Strapped across a sleeper's back on a cold watch, it does a bedroll's work — and the character who lends it out is making a visible choice.
- Walking it into a building tells the party something before anyone rolls: it is warmest where a household is, and it does not care whether that household is friendly.

## Hooks

The shield was the door-guard of a longhouse for six generations, hung on the inside wall and taken down twice. The family that owned it is scattered and its youngest is a cooper two towns over, who does not want it back and will not say the word "sold" aloud. She will, however, tell you the exact spot on the wall where it hung, and how the boss came to be flat.
