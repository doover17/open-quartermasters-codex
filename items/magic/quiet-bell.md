## Description

Two thumb-sized bells of dark bronze on separate cords, and neither one has a clapper — turn them over, shake them, hold them to the light, and there is nothing inside to make a sound with. Squeeze the body of one and the other rings, wherever it is, in whoever's hand. The bronze is worn brightest at the place a thumb goes.

## At the Table

- Split the party across a city, a siege line, or a plane and they still hold one channel: *rung, twice, now*. Agreeing on what the counts mean is a five-minute scene that pays out for a campaign.
- Whoever holds the twin holds a claim on you. Giving one away is a promise, and being given one is a leash you agreed to wear.

## Hooks

Bells are made in pairs and separated constantly — by death, by falling-out, by pawnbrokers who only ever get the one. The bell on this counter has rung four times since it came in: twice the week it arrived, once a month after that, and once yesterday afternoon. The pawnbroker has taken it out of the window.
