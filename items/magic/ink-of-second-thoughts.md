## Description

A squat bottle of ink so black it reads as a hole cut in the page, and it never entirely dries — run a thumb across a line written a decade ago and it will still smudge, faintly, and mark your skin. Writing with it feels ordinary right up until you set the pen down, at which point you already know whether the words are going to keep. Scribes describe that moment as being told something about yourself you hadn't asked about.

## At the Table

- A contract, a confession, a will: a page in this ink that survived the night was *meant*. That's a fact the party can carry into a courtroom or to a graveside.
- The player who writes a letter in it and finds it blank by morning has learned something the DM never had to narrate.

## Hooks

Chanceries won't accept documents in it and lovers swear by it, which tells you most of what you need to know. A magistrate three towns over keeps one page of it framed behind her bench, forty years old and unfaded: a confession to a killing, signed by a man her court acquitted. She hung it at the height where every defendant has to read it.
