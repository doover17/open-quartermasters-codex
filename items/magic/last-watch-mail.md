## Description

Fine riveted mail, oiled black, light enough over the shoulders that you keep checking it's still there. Every so often the rings shiver — a fast dry rustle across the chest and down one side, like rain starting on a roof — and then stop. It does this in barracks and on empty roads, before nothing and before everything, and no two veterans who have worn it agree on what it means.

## At the Table

- The best kind of unreliable narrator is an object. Rustle the mail when the party is safe and they will believe the room is dangerous; rustle it never, and they'll believe the opposite.
- A DM who wants the shiver to mean something specific should decide privately and never confirm it. Half the value is the argument at the table.

## Hooks

The shirt came off a wall-watch that was cut down to a man, and that man walked eleven miles to report it before he sat down and did not get up. It has been reissued four times since, always to someone standing a watch, and the garrison list beside its serial number has one column filled in and one left blank. The blank column is headed *returned*.
