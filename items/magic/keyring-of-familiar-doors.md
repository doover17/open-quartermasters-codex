## Description

A split iron ring hung with keys of every pattern — warded, lever, a flat brass thing for a cabinet, one carved from bone. Count them and you get a number; count them again and you get one more. The extra key is never the same key twice, and it is never the one you need, and it is always warm from a pocket.

## At the Table

- Every place the party has ever been welcomed is now a place they can return to at three in the morning. That reframes hospitality permanently, and players feel it.
- It rewards note-taking of the best kind: "did we open that door?" becomes the most important question in a heist.

## Hooks

The ring's power is a record of hospitality abused, which is why guild locksmiths cut a new pattern rather than sell one on. A house on the hill has had its locks changed four times this year, at ruinous cost, by an owner who cannot explain to the smith what he is protecting against. He has begun asking whether a door that is bricked up still counts as a door.
