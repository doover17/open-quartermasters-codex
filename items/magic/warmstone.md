## Description

A river-smoothed stone the size of a fist, gray-green and unremarkable, that warms in your hands within a breath and holds that warmth all night. Hold it near your face and it smells of woodsmoke and of bread coming out of an oven — a whole kitchen's worth of smell, off a bare stone, in a wet field, with nothing burning within ten miles. Most owners stop mentioning this part to strangers.

## At the Table

- The cold-weather arc's small mercy, and a real question about who gets it: one stone, five sleepers, and nobody wants to be the one who asks.
- Handed to a shivering prisoner, a sick child, or a scout who came in hostile, it does more diplomatic work than a speech.

## Hooks

They turn up in trade far more often than anyone makes them, and always singly. A drover found this one last winter in a snowbank on the high pass, still warm, with no tracks within a hundred yards: a bare circle of green grass in six feet of snow, the stone sitting in the middle of it, smelling of somebody's kitchen.
