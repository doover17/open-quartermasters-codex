## Description

A crude thing: a clay bowl, a bronze handle riveted on by somebody in a hurry, and a wick of twisted flax that has never shortened. It is older than the technique used to make it. It smells of the first rain falling on hot stone, and it smells of that in dry seasons, in sealed rooms, and underground — a smell people recognize before they have any idea why.

## At the Table

- The party cannot turn it off. Every scene requiring darkness, concealment, or a quiet approach now has to route around one member's cargo, and leaving it behind is its own decision.
- Its light does not end, so anything that needs the dark must come at it another way. That reframes a whole campaign's worth of enemies.

## Hooks

A city grew around where this lantern was set down, and grew in rings, and the oldest ring is the one whose streets all face the same point. It has been out of that city for two hundred years and the city has not gone dark. Whatever is burning in the shrine at the center is burning without a wick, and the keepers change the oil weekly, and the oil is never gone.
