## Description

A tall yew bow, back and belly darkened to the color of strong tea, with a nock carved into the shape of a hooded bird's head. Arrows loosed from it are always found again — in the grass, in the mud, in the river, days later and a valley over, but always found, and always by the person who loosed them. Fletchers who have handled it say the arrows come back with their own fletching intact and somebody else's mud on them.

## At the Table

- Ammunition stops being a ledger line and starts being a slow, strange mail service: every shot the party takes will come back to them eventually.
- The arrow that comes back before it should — same day, wrong direction, wrong mud — is an encounter with no monster in it yet.

## Hooks

The bow is named for a falconer who is said to have loosed one arrow at something over a lake and then waited on that shore for nine years. There is a shingle beach where a hut used to be and the local children still turn up arrows there, all one fletching, all pointing inland. The count is somewhere past four hundred.
