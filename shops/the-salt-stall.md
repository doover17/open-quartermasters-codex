## The Shop

Three trestles under a striped awning at the wet end of the market, where the runoff from the fish rows goes past in a channel and everyone has learned to stand upwind. The salt is heaped in open cones, gray and coarse, and the jerky hangs on wires overhead so you duck to get served. Everything here smells of brine and pepper, and the trestle boards are white with a decade of spilled salt worked into the grain.

## The Merchant

Bett Harrow has run the stall since her mother did, and she prices out loud, to the whole row, as a kind of performance. Wide-shouldered, sunburnt to the collar, with a thumb she lost to a curing knife and shows to children on request. She wants the guild seat that the fishmongers keep voting down. *"Taste it first. Nobody's ever bought twice off a stall that wouldn't let them taste it first."*

## Behind the Counter

- Haggling is the entertainment and she'll go three rounds happily, but the last price is always a round number and she will not go under it in front of an audience.
- Buy in bulk and she throws in the spice tin refill free; ask her to weigh light for the tally and the conversation ends there, loudly.
- She knows which caravans are overdue by what she can't restock, and she'll tell anyone who asks in a friendly way.

## Hooks

Bett takes empty jars back for a copper each and refills them, so the same jars have been going round the town for years. One came back last month sealed, which is not how they come back, and heavy. She has it under the trestle and she has not opened it, because the wax on it is the gray-green wax the town used for burial goods two generations ago and nobody alive here seals anything that way.
