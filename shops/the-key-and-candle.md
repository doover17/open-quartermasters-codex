## The Shop

Small, bright, and startlingly clean, with a bench under the window where the work happens in full view of the street — deliberately, because a locksmith who works out of sight is a locksmith nobody trusts. The wall behind the counter is keys: hundreds, on numbered hooks, each tagged in the same tiny hand. It smells of brass filings, lamp oil, and the faint vinegar of the paste she sells by the tin.

## The Merchant

Ivette Sarn is bonded, licensed, and audited twice a year, and she volunteers all three facts before you've asked. Narrow spectacles, a jeweler's loupe on a ribbon, sleeves pinned back off the wrist. She wants the guild to stop apprenticing boys who can't read a ward diagram. *"Fixed prices, posted prices, same for everyone — that's not stubbornness, that's the bond. Argue me down and I'd have to write it in the book and explain why."*

## Behind the Counter

- She will not move on price and cannot, and she'll show you the clause in the bond if you press, which most people find more interesting than the discount would have been.
- She refuses work on any lock whose owner isn't present, no matter the fee, and has turned away titled customers over it.
- She'll teach any customer to maintain their own lock for free, at the bench, for as long as it takes. She regards this as advertising.

## Hooks

Hook forty-one on the key wall has held the same key for six years: cut, paid for, tagged, never collected. Ivette has written to the address on the order twice. The house is occupied, the occupants are pleasant, and they say nobody there ordered a key. The tag is in her own hand and the order is in her book, and the signature in the book is one she has never been able to read.
