## The Shop

A long room lit badly on purpose, with the goods on shelves by category rather than quality, so the boots are all together and you can see exactly how many pairs of boots come through a place like this. It smells of other people's houses. Everything carries a paper tag on a string, and every tag has two dates on it: taken in, and redeemable until.

## The Merchant

Aurel Greyhand is thin, courteous, and entirely without small talk, and he has never once in eleven years mispriced an item. Half-moon spectacles, a black apron, hands that handle everything as though it still belongs to someone else. He wants nothing you can sell him, which is most of the problem with dealing with him. *"That is the price. You are welcome to think it low. I am not going to argue about it, and I am not going to change it."*

## Behind the Counter

- Attempting to haggle ends the transaction — not angrily, but completely. He puts the item back on the shelf and waits for you to leave.
- He pays measurably better than anyone in the quarter for goods with a name, initials, or a maker's mark still on them, and he does not explain why.
- He will tell any customer, unprompted and accurately, everything he knows about where a piece came from. Most customers stop asking after the first time.

## Hooks

Six shoddy healer's kits have come in this year with the owner's initials scraped off the lid — not sanded, scraped, with the edge of the salve tin. Aurel has bought every one at above the shelf rate and shelved none of them. They are in a crate behind the counter, in order of arrival, and he has started asking sellers which field hospital they trained at before he counts out the coin.
