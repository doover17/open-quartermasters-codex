## The Shop

The heat reaches the street before the noise does, and the noise reaches it well before you find the door. Inside is one bay, one fire, and a wall of finished work hung by size — pitons, hooks, hinges, a rack of wedges — with the floor swept in a clean arc around the anvil and nowhere else. There are two anvils. Only one is in use; the older one stands against the back wall with a cloth over it.

## The Merchant

Corran Ashe took over the forge at twenty-six and has never explained the shop's name to a customer who didn't already know. Forearms like cable, a burn scar the shape of a hand across one wrist, and a habit of finishing your sentence about what you need and being right. He wants an apprentice who will last more than a season. *"I can make it, I can mend it, or I can tell you honestly to buy it elsewhere. Which are we doing?"*

## Behind the Counter

- He'll knock a fair amount off for anyone who brings scrap worth melting, and he weighs it in front of you without being asked.
- Repairs come before sales. A customer who wants something new while somebody's plough is in pieces on the bench will be invited to wait, and will wait.
- Ask about the covered anvil and he answers pleasantly and completely — it was his father's, it rings flat, and he keeps it for the sound of not using it.

## Hooks

Corran has been commissioned four times this year to fit iron strongboxes with a second, internal hasp — one that locks from the inside. All four commissions came through different agents, all four boxes went to the same address on the hill, and the last agent asked whether the work could be done with the box already closed.
