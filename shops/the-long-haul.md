## The Shop

A long shed at the fork where the north and the mountain roads part, with both doors open in all weather so the through-draft keeps the canvas from souring. Gear hangs at the height it's used at — packs at shoulder, cord at hand, bedrolls overhead — and there is a bench by the stove where nobody buys anything and everybody sits. Behind the counter is a board of route notes on ribbons, updated in five hands.

## The Merchant

Odalie Fenn came out here to write a book about the pass and has been outfitting other people's expeditions for nineteen years instead. Weather-cut face, one eye that waters in cold, a pencil permanently behind her ear. She wants somebody to bring back an accurate count of the cairns above the third switchback. *"I'll sell you what you ask for. But sit down first and tell me where you're going, because I'd rather sell you the right thing twice than the wrong thing once."*

## Behind the Counter

- Her prices are frontier prices and she won't apologize for them, but she'll spend an hour talking a party out of gear they don't need, which costs her money and she does it anyway.
- Everyone who buys is asked to add a line to the ribbon board on their way back through. About half do.
- She trades hard for trail news and pays in kind — a party with a good report leaves better equipped than they could afford.

## Hooks

The ribbon board has four ribbons in the same hand, dated across one season, each reporting the same stretch of the mountain road in careful detail. The fourth was added three weeks ago. Odalie remembers outfitting that party once, at the start of the season, and remembers them clearly — because they never came back through, and she has been checking the board every morning since to see whether a fifth has appeared.
