## The Shop

Two rooms of shelves with no visible organizing principle, lit by four glowvials the owner shakes each morning as a ritual, and cold in a way the fireplace never fixes. Things here are displayed with their prices facing away, so you have to pick them up. The floor creaks in one particular spot and every regular steps over it without thinking about it.

## The Merchant

Pol Rennick buys anything that does something and sells it to whoever seems likeliest to find out what. Sixty, delighted, with reading glasses on a chain and three more pairs elsewhere in the shop. He wants to be present, once, when somebody works out what a thing is actually for. *"Don't ask me what it does — ask me what it did, for the last person, and I'll tell you everything they told me and nothing I made up."*

## Behind the Counter

- He haggles enthusiastically and badly, and a customer who is any good at it will get a bargain and a cheerful loser.
- Every item comes with its provenance recited from memory, and he is scrupulous about separating what he knows from what he was told.
- He'll let a customer test anything on the premises, including things he probably shouldn't, and he watches with his hands behind his back.

## Hooks

Three of the four homing compasses on the shelf have been sold and returned — different buyers, different years, each returning it within a month and refusing a refund. Pol has checked them against each other on the counter. All three needles point the same way, at the same thing, and none of the three buyers named the same place when they bought it.
