## The Shop

A narrow frontage with a wide back, and the back is where the smell lives: sulfur, mint, hot cork, and under all of it something faintly sweet that regulars stop noticing by their third visit. The front room is almost bare — a counter, a slate of prices, and a single shelf of glowvials left shaken so the shop is always lit green from one corner. Everything else is fetched.

## The Merchant

Nobody has established whether Mother Cinder is a name, a title, or the third woman to hold it; the current one is perhaps forty and answers to it without comment. Ink-stained to the second knuckle, spectacles pushed up into gray-streaked hair, and a way of listening to a request with her eyes closed. She wants a supply of beetle-shell that doesn't come through the harbor. *"The slate is the price. I don't move it for lords and I won't move it for you, so let's skip that part and talk about what you actually need it to do."*

## Behind the Counter

- She won't haggle, but she will substitute: tell her the problem instead of the product and she'll often sell you something cheaper that solves it.
- She asks what an acid vial is for, every time, and remembers the answer. Buyers who won't say get served politely and slowly, and get the slate price on everything forever.
- Spent glassware is worth a copper each back, which means her regulars are all faintly rattling.

## Hooks

She keeps her tanglefoot sap under the counter, not because it's illegal but because this season's gourds grip harder than any batch she's bought in twenty years, and she doesn't trust the difference. She has written to the herbalist twice. The second letter came back unopened with the seal intact and a different hand's note on the outside: *she is not at the grove.*
