## The Shop

Warm the moment the door shuts, and the warmth has a texture — rendered fat, pine resin, and the flat mineral smell of a great deal of burning. Candles hang in ranks from drying racks that run the length of the ceiling, dipped and re-dipped, and the floor under them is a soft archaeology of drips gone brown. A boy sits at the back trimming wicks and does not look up.

## The Merchant

Ivo Renn sells light by the crate and thinks retail is a hobby he can't afford to give up. Round, quick, permanently sleeved to the elbow, with a chandler's forearms and a bookkeeper's suspicion. He wants the watch-house contract that goes to tender in the spring. *"Torches are two coppers because torches are worth two coppers. If you want the thing that works, it's on the shelf behind me and it costs what it costs."*

## Behind the Counter

- He'll cut a real discount for volume and none at all for charm, and he'll tell you which you're attempting.
- He warns every buyer off the sputter torches, in the same words, and sells them anyway by the hundred. He regards this as fully honest and it mostly is.
- Bring back wax ends and he weighs them into credit — which is why half the street's servants know him and he knows what half the street burns.

## Hooks

The funeral-resin thread in this winter's fire-logs came from Ivo's own stock and he can account for every sweeping that went into them, which is what troubles him. He has started burning one log a week himself, alone, after closing, working back through his suppliers in order. He is four weeks in. The smell is getting stronger, and he has begun leaving the shutters open while he does it.
