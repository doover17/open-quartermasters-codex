## The Shop

You smell the pits before the building — lime, bark liquor, and the deep animal sweetness underneath that nobody who works here can detect any more. The trade room is one step up out of the mud, hung floor to roof with hides in grades, and the counter is a single slab worn into a shallow dish where a century of goods have been pushed across it. Coin is uncommon here. Most business crosses that dish in both directions.

## The Merchant

Hesk keeps the post with two grown sons who do the pits, and she does the talking and all of the arithmetic. Broad, unhurried, with cracked hands she keeps greased and a way of pricing a hide by looking at it from across the room. She wants a smith to settle within a day's ride, and has offered land. *"I'll give you goods or I'll give you coin, and the goods are better. That's not a trick, it's what coin's worth out here."*

## Behind the Counter

- She'll haggle cheerfully and at length, and the whole post treats it as the evening's entertainment; a customer who won't play is served fairly and thought less of.
- Barter beats coin every time, and she'll say so and show her working — she prices in salt, iron, and thread, in that order.
- New arrivals get their boots looked at, uninvited, and told exactly how long they'll last out here. She is never wrong and rarely tactful.

## Hooks

One trapper comes down twice a season with good pelts and pays for his resupply in coin instead of trade, which nobody out here does. The coin is old — older than the post, older than the road — and it is not worn. Hesk has been taking it, because it spends, and she has started keeping his pieces separate from the till in a tin she does not put on the shelf.
